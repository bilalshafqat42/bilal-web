# Project: Bilal Shafqat — freelance website (UAE)

This file gives context for this codebase. Claude Code (or any AI assistant working in this repo) should read this before making changes, so the site build stays consistent with the brief below.

## Who this is for

Bilal is a UAE-based freelancer building his own personal brand website. He positions himself as a full-fledged, one-person digital marketing and product shop, not a narrow specialist. He personally covers:

- Performance development, web and mobile (builds the sites/apps himself)
- Design, web, app, and social media
- Paid campaigns / ads management (Meta, Google, TikTok)
- Automation (workflow / marketing automation)
- Performance marketing (results-driven reporting)

Positioning line: one person doing what usually takes an agency, no handoffs between departments, single point of contact. The site's main goal is to win clients and read as professional, not just look impressive.

16. **Hosting, sold as a managed care plan rather than as hosting.** Bilal's request, 2026-08-26. The recurring-revenue case is strong and it attaches naturally to every site he builds, but this is the first service on the list that carries **ongoing operational liability**, so it needs framing and boundaries the audits do not.
    - **Do not sell "hosting" as a line item.** Reselling Vercel, DigitalOcean or AWS means marking up someone else's infrastructure at thin margin, while inheriting all of the responsibility. Sell a **managed care plan** instead, where hosting is one component: hosting + SSL + automated backups + uptime monitoring + dependency and security updates + a small monthly change allowance + a monthly performance report. Same underlying cost, several times the perceived value, and it sets support expectations explicitly.
    - **This is the recurring wrapper the rest of Phase 3 is missing.** Every one-off in item 15 decays: WCAG conformance breaks with the next content edit, tracking pixels break when platforms change, Core Web Vitals regress as pages grow.     - **Risks to accept deliberately before offering it**, because they are real and none of them apply to an audit:
      - **Uptime becomes his problem, permanently.** A solo freelancer has no on-call rotation. A client's site going down mid-campaign at 3am is his to answer. Audits and builds end; hosting never does.
      - **Scope creep is near-guaranteed.** Hosting clients raise unrelated issues ("my email is broken"). The care plan must state in writing what is *not* included.
      - **Stack choice changes the risk profile enormously.** Next.js and static sites on a managed platform are low-maintenance. Hosting WordPress means inheriting every plugin vulnerability on every client site. Recommend restricting the offer to sites he built himself, at least initially, rather than taking over arbitrary legacy installs.
      - **An SLA is required, even a modest one.** Without a stated response window and maintenance window, expectations default to 24/7. State them plainly, and price accordingly.
    - **Data residency and UAE PDPL need checking, not assuming.** Some UAE clients — regulated sectors and anything government-adjacent especially — may require data to stay in-region, which constrains hosting location. This interacts with UAE PDPL obligations around personal data in forms and CRMs. Confirm the position with a qualified professional before making any commitment in writing; do not state compliance in page copy.
    - **International note** (see the cross-cutting section below): hosting itself travels fine, since the platforms are global. Support **hours** do not. A UK client overlaps most of Bilal's working day; a US Pacific client does not. Any SLA offered to North American clients should be async-friendly and say so, rather than implying live coverage.

    - **The remaining constraint is memory, not CPU.** The two full-page captures are 1916×6555 and 1920×6682, needing **~48MB and ~49MB of RAM** each to decode regardless of file size. Fix is dimensional, not compression: export screenshots at **1600px wide** and split very tall pages into 2-3 sections rather than one continuous strip.
